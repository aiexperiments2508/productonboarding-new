What the sweep found

The engine is well built. Accessibility naming is close to exemplary, the deterministic validator holds its hash under repeat runs, the agent and MCP instrumentation is genuinely useful, and the copy explains its own limits rather than hiding them. The defects cluster in three places: things that are counted twice and disagree, controls that look live but do nothing, and a layout that only works on a tall screen.

The one to fix first

Two CRITICAL approvals sit in /api/approvals/pending — one of them an allergen declaration moving from ['milk'] to ['gluten','milk','egg'] on a regulated product. The sidebar badge counts them. The Review & Audit screen, which exists to action them, says “Nothing awaiting a decision.” They only become reachable if you happen to re-run that case in your own browser session. A human-in-the-loop gate that a second human cannot see is the highest-consequence finding here.

Method. Browser-driven exploration with DOM and network instrumentation. Counts were checked against the API rather than read off the screen; contrast was measured by canvas pixel readback because the palette is authored in oklch(). Where a finding depended on a CSS override to reach a control, it was re-verified on a clean page load with no overrides. Severity reflects consequence in a compliance workflow, not effort to fix.
