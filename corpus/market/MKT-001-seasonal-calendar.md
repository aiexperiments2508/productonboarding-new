# Seasonal and Festive Calendar

What sells, when, and why. This exists so that a claim about why somebody would
buy a product *now* has something behind it. A differentiator written from a
model's general impression of summer is an ungrounded claim on a page a shopper
reads; one written from this document cites a passage.

## Seasons

- **Spring (Mar-May).** Cleaning, allergy season. Air treatment peaks in April
  as pollen counts rise; purifier searches roughly double against the February
  baseline. Kitchen small appliances are flat.
- **Summer (Jun-Aug).** Cooling, outdoor eating, travel. Fans peak. Snacking
  shifts to portable formats - single bars over multipacks - and shoppers read
  the pack in the hand rather than on the shelf.
- **Autumn (Sep-Nov).** Back-to-routine, then the gifting run-up. Audio peaks
  in November. Breakfast and lunchbox formats recover.
- **Winter (Dec-Feb).** Gifting, then January health. Kettles peak in December
  as gifts; granola and high-fibre formats peak in January.

## Festivities

- **Ramadan and Eid.** Dates move each year. Snack multipacks and shareable
  formats outsell singles by a wide margin; ingredient transparency matters more
  than usual and the ingredient panel image is read.
- **Diwali.** Gifting across home and kitchen. Presentation and pack condition
  carry more weight than specification.
- **Christmas.** Audio and kitchen gifting. Price-marked packs underperform;
  shoppers read a gift as a gift.
- **New Year.** Health, air quality and fitness. Purifiers and high-fibre foods
  both lift; "low energy" reads as thrift rather than as environmentalism.
- **School holidays and the festival season (Jul-Aug).** The longest run of the
  year without a gifting peak, and the one where the shelf does the selling.
  Portable and single-serve snack formats outsell multipacks; shoppers buy for
  the day rather than for the week. Outdoor and travel occasions lift portable
  audio, and heat lifts fans and air treatment - both bought on noise level
  first, because they are running in a room somebody is sleeping in.
- **Back-to-school (late Aug).** Lunchbox formats recover a fortnight before
  term. Allergen declarations are read more carefully here than at any other
  point in the year, because the person reading is buying for a child they will
  not be with at lunchtime.

## Regional notes

- **Urban, small dwellings.** Coverage area and noise level are the deciding
  attributes for air treatment; wattage is read as a running-cost proxy.
- **Coastal and humid.** Filter life is asked about more than filter type.
- **Cold-climate.** Kettle speed and capacity outrank aesthetics.

## How to use this

A differentiator may lean on this document only where the product's own record
supports it. "Comfortable in summer" needs a summer passage *and* an attribute
that makes it comfortable. The calendar says when a claim is relevant; it never
says whether it is true.
