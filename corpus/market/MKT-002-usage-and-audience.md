# Popular Usage and Who Buys It

How these categories are actually used, from returns commentary, review mining
and store feedback. A value differentiator is grounded when it names a use this
document records and an attribute the record holds.

## Air treatment (PRD-01, PRD-06)

Bought for bedrooms first and living rooms second. The two questions asked in
store are "will I hear it at night" and "what does it cost to leave on". Sound
level and rated power therefore carry the positioning; filter type is checked by
a minority and coverage area is checked by almost everybody who measures a room
first.

Sleep use is the dominant pattern. A purifier quiet enough to run overnight is
described as such by reviewers far more often than it is described as powerful.

## Snacks and granola (PRD-02, PRD-05)

Bars are lunchbox and desk-drawer purchases; multipacks are household
provisioning. Granola is breakfast at home and, increasingly, an afternoon
yoghurt topping. Fibre content is read by a health-motivated minority and
ignored by everyone else, but that minority reads it carefully and complains
loudly when it moves.

Allergen information is read by a small number of people for whom it is the only
thing on the pack that matters.

## Earbuds (PRD-03)

Commuting and exercise, roughly evenly. Battery life decides the commuting
purchase and fit decides the exercise one. Sound quality is discussed after
purchase and rarely before it.

## Kettles (PRD-04)

Speed and capacity. Bought to replace one that failed, so the purchase is
urgent and comparison is shallow - the first listing that answers "how fast and
how much does it hold" tends to win.

## Writing a differentiator from this

Name the use, then the attribute that supports it, then the season or region if
one applies. "Quiet enough to run overnight in a bedroom" is grounded by a sound
level. "Best purifier available" is not grounded by anything.
