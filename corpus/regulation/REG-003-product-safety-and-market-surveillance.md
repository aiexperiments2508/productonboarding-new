# General Product Safety and Market Surveillance

Applies to every product offered for sale, in every category. The category
regulations - `REG-001` for food, `REG-002` for appliances, `REG-004` for
textiles, `REG-005` for cosmetics - say what a listing must declare. This one
says who may stop a sale, and what happens when they do.

## The general obligation

A product placed on the market SHALL be safe. Compliance with a category
regulation is evidence of safety and is not a substitute for it: a listing that
carries every mandatory particular correctly is still withdrawn if the product
behind it is unsafe.

## Withdrawal and recall

A market surveillance authority may direct that a product be **withdrawn** from
sale, **recalled** from customers, or both.

- A **withdrawal** stops further supply. The listing comes down; stock already
  sold stays sold.
- A **recall** additionally requires stock already with customers to be
  returned. It reaches a set of people a withdrawal does not, and it is
  therefore a heavier instruction and not merely a stronger word.

A direction under this section is **effective on receipt**. It is not
conditional on the retailer's own assessment, it does not wait for the
supplier's response, and it is not satisfied by preparing a corrected
specification. A product under a withdrawal direction SHALL NOT be offered for
sale on any channel while the direction stands.

## What a direction is not

It is not a correction. Nothing in the product record need be wrong for a
direction to be issued, and correcting the record does not discharge one. A
retailer that responded to a withdrawal notice by amending the copy and
republishing would have complied with nothing.

This distinction is the reason a direction cannot be cleared by content work.
The remedy for a wrong value is a right value. There is no sentence that makes
a withdrawn product sellable.

## Channels that cannot be recalled

Where a product has been advertised in a medium that cannot be retrieved - a
printed catalogue already distributed, a shelf-edge label already in the aisle
- the obligation is not discharged by taking down what can be taken down.

- A printed advertisement carries an **erratum** obligation: open, owned and
  dated, discharged when the correction is published.
- A shelf-edge label carries a **reprint** obligation, discharged when the
  label is physically replaced.
- A search facet directing shoppers to a withdrawn product SHALL be withdrawn.

Reporting a print channel as "redacted" when 214,000 catalogues are in the
world is a false statement about a legal obligation, and is the one report this
system may never make. `INC-2026-002` is the incident behind that sentence.

## Restoration

A direction may be lifted. Restoration is a separate decision from withdrawal
and SHALL be recorded as its own act: the authority that stopped the sale is
the authority that resumes it, and a retailer restoring a listing because a
supplier has assured it the matter is closed has not been told by anybody who
can tell it.
