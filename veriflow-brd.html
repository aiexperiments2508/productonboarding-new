02
Business Context

Why this problem matters

Retail product data arrives from suppliers in inconsistent formats, while channel, taxonomy and compliance requirements vary by market. The operating environment spans supplier spreadsheets, documents, images, taxonomy, channel rules, regulatory attributes and correction feeds. Evidence can arrive asynchronously, be corrected later, or conflict across systems — so any solution must preserve state, provenance and prior actions as each case evolves.

Complexity the business must accept

The solution must reconcile changing evidence across multiple systems, maintain long-running state, support real approvals and continue safely through partial failures. Material changes must trigger targeted re-evaluation rather than silently preserving an outdated conclusion.

Business need, in one line: convert fragmented supplier inputs into governed, source-linked and channel-ready product records — with uncertainty made explicit and every action fully accountable.

05
Scope

In scope / out of scope

✓

Ingesting and reconciling supplier spreadsheets, documents, images, taxonomy, channel rules, regulatory attributes and correction feeds into one evidence graph.

✓

Detecting and surfacing uncertainty (missing, stale, duplicate, contradictory) rather than silently resolving it.

✓

Dynamically planning the next evidence request, enrichment, validation, escalation or publish action per case.

✓

Governance: approval gates for regulated/material claims, full provenance and audit replay.

✓

Propagating late corrections to exactly the affected fields, approvals and published channels (the "finale inject" capability).

✓

Deploying alongside an existing PIM/ERP/channel stack as a governance and orchestration layer, using representative synthetic data.

Out of scope (v1)

  • Replacing an enterprise's existing PIM, ERP, or channel/commerce platforms outright.
  • Physical logistics, warehousing, inventory allocation, or payment processing.
  • Training foundation AI models from scratch — Veriflow consumes configurable, swappable model/provider endpoints.
  • Live connections to confidential enterprise or partner systems — v1 uses representative synthetic data and mock interfaces per Problem Statement 05's data-availability guidance.
  • Consumer-facing storefront experiences — Veriflow's experience layer is an internal operator/reviewer workspace.

Assumptions & Dependencies

Assumptions, constraints & dependencies

Assumptions

  • Representative synthetic supplier spreadsheets, documents, images, taxonomy, channel rules, regulatory attributes and correction feeds are sufficient to demonstrate the complete solution; no live confidential enterprise data is required.
  • Where live enterprise/partner access is unavailable, mock interfaces or simulated services stand in for real integrations, but the solution itself must behave as a deployable product.
  • Model/AI providers are configurable and swappable — no specific vendor or architecture is mandated.

Constraints

  • Must be demonstrable end-to-end within the National Finale's five-day build and evaluation window.
  • Regulated claims and material product assertions require configured human or policy approval — this cannot be bypassed by automation.
  • Technology choices are free, but must be defensible on appropriateness, implementation quality and business impact, not novelty alone.

Dependencies

  • Availability of synthetic data sets covering edge cases, corrections, failure responses and approval states.
  • Configured connectors (real or mocked) to taxonomy, channel-rule and regulatory-attribute sources.
  • An approvals policy configuration owned by Compliance/Category stakeholders before go-live.

Governance

Governance & compliance requirements

Non-negotiable governance requirement

Regulated claims and material product assertions require configured human or policy approval — in every environment, for every channel, with no override path that bypasses this gate.

Controls must exist in the running product, not only in documentation. Section 09 of the AI Friday Engineering Guide requires that teams explicitly define what AI can do, what requires human approval, and what AI is prohibited from doing:

Control categoryApplied in Veriflow
Allowed AI assistanceRetrieve, interpret, summarize, compare, generate, recommend and coordinate approved enrichment/mapping activities.
Human-approved actionsRegulated/material claim changes, publishing a corrected specification, overriding a hypothesis, exceptional-case resolution.
Prohibited AI actionsUnauthorized data access, bypassing approval gates, invoking unapproved tools, making irreversible publish decisions without confirmation.
Escalation triggersInsufficient evidence, low confidence, contradictory information, policy conflict, security concern, or tool failure.
AuditabilityEvery AI recommendation, evidence used, tool/action requested, human approval/override and final outcome is recorded and replayable.
